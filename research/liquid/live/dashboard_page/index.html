What the server does each weekday

  1. 08:00Starts, checks the account is flat, builds the day plan: noise area, size, the 12 check times.
  2. 09:30The session opens. The plan's upper and lower bounds are fixed for the whole day.
  3. 10:00–15:30Every half hour: enter if the close is above the upper bound and above VWAP; otherwise re-price the stop.
  4. 15:59Sells anything still held. A separate guard flattens the account if the runner dies.
  5. 16:20Evening report: execution cost against the SIP mid, the research replay, the three gates.
  6. thenPublishes this page's data to Google Drive.

    How C3 trades

    C3 is the long side of the "noise area" intraday momentum rule from Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon, Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P 500 ETF (SPY) (2024), with the paper's own parameters. The idea: most intraday wandering stays inside a band set by how far SPY usually strays from its open by that minute. A close outside the band, backed by the day's volume-weighted price, tends to keep going until the close.

    1 · The noise area

    For each minute of the day, σ is the average absolute move from the open to that minute over the prior 14 sessions. Upper bound = max(open, yesterday's close) × (1 + σ); lower bound = min(open, yesterday's close) × (1 − σ). Both are known at 09:30.

    2 · When it looks

    Only on the half hour, from 10:00 to 15:30: twelve checks on a full day. Between checks it does nothing but let the stop work.

    3 · Entry

    Buy when the minute close is above the upper bound and above VWAP. A limit order 0.1% above that close caps the price paid. Long only: a break below the lower bound is no signal here.

    4 · Size

    Weight = 2% ÷ the daily volatility of the last 14 sessions, at most 4× equity, so calm days carry more. On the paper account the order is also capped by buying power.

    5 · Exit

    A stop at max(upper bound, VWAP), re-set at every check, so it trails up with VWAP. Whatever is left is sold at 15:59. Stopped out, it may enter again at a later check.

      Why this is paper trading and not a result

      The liquid track pre-registered three cells on SPY and QQQ. None passed: C3 had the best Sharpe and a positive alpha, but failed the guard that doubles costs (a Sharpe of 0.62 against SPY's 0.68), and its Sharpe lead over SPY has an interval that includes zero. Paper-trading it is a labelled deviation chosen to measure one thing the backtest cannot: what a round trip really costs on this broker. A G-cost pass leads to a decision (read the confirmation window, or a small real-money pilot), not to "C3 works".

      Costs

      Alpaca charges no commission. The research cost model charges half a cent a share on each side for the spread, one more cent a share when a stop fills, and the SEC, FINRA TAF and CAT fees: about 1 bp per round trip at dev prices. Live cost is measured per trip against the SIP mid at the decision minute, so it includes the delay from decision to fill.

      How the live numbers are made

      The runner reads minute bars from a hybrid feed (SIP for bars at least 16 minutes old, IEX for the newest), places a limit buy and a stop, and logs every check and order to a ledger. After the close the report re-reads the day's SIP bars, replays the research rule on them, prices each fill against the SIP mid and updates the gates. The server then writes two compressed files to Google Drive, which this page decodes in your browser with your own Drive access.

      Paper fills are simulated by Alpaca and can be kinder than real ones, so a G-cost pass on paper is an upper bound on what real money would see.

      Running the server

      The server is a Google Cloud e2-micro in us-east1 (free-tier shape) with a systemd timer at 08:00 ET on weekdays; it reaches only Alpaca's paper endpoint and Google Drive. To stop trading at once, create the KILL file on the server; to stop the schedule, disable the timer. The spending alert is set at about $5 a month; Google Cloud has no hard cap for virtual machines.

      Research figures come from files built by research/liquid/live/dashboard.py (--dev) from the dev cache. Live figures come from the server's nightly files. Sources, beside the page, shows each file and its code.
