# Stage 3 literature scan -- intraday small-cap long-only, 1-minute bars

Date: 2026-11-04. Method: direct search with several phrasings, citation chaining from seed papers
(Semantic Scholar), verified metadata only. Results are quoted only where traceable to an abstract,
a publisher page or a table that was read in this session. Anything not verified is marked UNVERIFIED.

Program context (for the "Relevance" column): ~10 pre-open-screened low-priced names per day, group
drifts about -0.9% open-to-close; round trip ~1.3% of notional; LightGBM on ~100 minute features with
triple-barrier labels; gross edge ~+0.7% per selected trade; winner's curse ~1 pp; losses concentrated
in collapsing names; hold-to-close beats 30-minute exits gross.

Plan hypotheses referenced below: H1 fewer/longer trades, H2 selection-aware entry, H3 setups +
meta-labeling, H4 learned dynamic exit, H5 day-level gate.

## Ideas worth borrowing

1. **Hold into the last half hour by default** (Bogousslavsky, JFE: size and illiquidity premia are realised in the last 30 minutes). This informs **H1** (fewer, longer trades) and **H4**. An exit learned in H4 should beat "hold to close" and not just "30-minute exit", because hold-to-close is the default the literature favours for small, illiquid names.
2. **Delay entry and price the open as a cost** (Berkman et al., JFQA 2012: at the open the implicit cost of buying attention stocks exceeds the half-spread). This informs **H2** and **H5**. Test a no-entry window after the open and use the overnight gap as a gate variable.
3. **Shrink before you select** (Smith and Winkler, Management Science 2006; Andrews, Kitagawa and McCloskey, "Inference on Winners"). This informs **H2**. Replace "predicted return above cost" with "selection-adjusted lower bound above cost". The shrinkage grows with the number of candidates competed over in the same day.
4. **Conformal selection with the threshold set at cost** (Jin and Candes, JMLR 2023). This informs **H2**. Use conformal p-values for H0: realised net return <= 0, then apply BH at a chosen FDR. Calibrate on whole held-out days, not on overlapping minutes. For the live, irreversible stream, follow the online variants (Liu et al., AAAI 2025; Huo et al., NeurIPS 2024, which adds budget and diversity constraints).
5. **Calibrate the secondary model before a fixed threshold** (Meyer, Barziy and Joubert, JFDS 2023: calibration helps fixed sizing rules significantly). This informs **H3** and **H2**. The 1 pp optimism is partly a calibration problem, so apply isotonic calibration on held-out days before any cost threshold.
6. **Meta-label a small set of rule setups instead of every minute** (Joubert, JFDS 2022; Meyer, Joubert and Alfeus, JFDS 2022). This informs **H3**. The rule entries lose on their own, but meta-labeling exists to filter a primary signal's false positives. Use one secondary model pooled across setup families, with the setup id as a feature (the strategy-driven architecture).
7. **Treat the exit as a regression of continuation value, solved backward from the close** (Longstaff and Schwartz, RFS 2001; Becker, Cheridito and Jentzen, JMLR 2019 as the neural upgrade). This informs **H4**. At each check time, fit E[return to close | state] on held paths with a GBDT and exit when it is below zero after cost. Hold-to-close is the boundary condition.
8. **Drop or widen the fixed 1-sigma stop** (Kaminski and Lo, JFM 2014: under a random walk a stop-loss always lowers expected return). This informs **H1** and **H4**. Our stop-out rate equals the random-walk rate, so the stop adds turnover without adding information. The exception to test is collapsing names, where losses may have momentum.
9. **Learn against net utility, ranked within the day** (Jensen, Kelly, Malamud and Pedersen, RFS in press; Poh et al., JFDS 2021). This informs **H1** and **H2**. Use LightGBM lambdarank grouped by day, with gains equal to the net-of-cost return. Cost-blind forecasts load on short-lived signals, and those are the ones a 1.3% cost kills.
10. **Name-level veto on herding and supply** (Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz, JF 2022: retail herding forecasts -4.7% to -19.6% over 20 days; Billett, Floros and Garfinkel, JFQA 2019, on ATM offerings). This informs **H5**. Flag names with extreme retail-attention spikes or an active shelf or ATM programme from EDGAR filings that predate the session.

## Warnings from the literature

- **ML gross edge in small names does not survive costs.** Avramov, Cheng and Metzker (Management Science 2023) find that ML profits come mainly from microcaps, distressed firms and high-volatility states, that excluding any of these "considerably attenuates" profitability, and that trading costs cut it further. Our ~0.7% gross against 1.3% cost matches this pattern.
- **Cost-blind forecasts favour signals that die after cost.** Jensen et al. find that standard ML forecasts load on short-lived, small-scale characteristics and earn weak net returns. Retuning the same pointwise model is unlikely to close a 0.6 pp gap.
- **Day-trading skill is rare and fees are decisive.** In Barber et al. (JFM 2014), fewer than 1% of Taiwan day traders were predictably profitable net of fees. The best 500 earned 61.3 bps/day gross and 37.9 bps/day net, so their whole gross edge is below half our single-round-trip cost. In Chague et al. (SSRN 3423101), 97% of persistent Brazilian day traders lost money.
- **The ORB "success" does not cover our universe or costs.** Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (SFI RP 24-98) filter to stocks above $5 with more than 1M volume, model commission only (no spread found in the text), and trade long and short. A non-peer-reviewed replication of the QQQ ORB reports that the gross result reproduces but the net is zero (UNVERIFIED secondary source).
- **Stops under a random walk cost return.** Kaminski and Lo (JFM 2014) show that simple stop-loss rules always lower expected return when returns are unforecastable. Each stop-out also pays a full round-trip cost.
- **The open is expensive in attention names beyond the quoted spread.** Berkman et al. (JFQA 2012) find the implicit cost at the open often exceeds the effective half-spread, so a 1.3% cost model may understate the real cost of early entries.
- **Attention buying loses on average.** In Barber et al. (JF 2022), intense Robinhood buying forecasts negative returns. A long-only book in screened attention names starts on the losing side of that flow.
- **Minute-level reversal is partly bid-ask bounce.** Heston et al. (JF 2010) attribute short-term reversal to sub-hour liquidity imbalances and bounce. Labels and features built on last-trade prices therefore overstate the predictability we can trade at the quote.
- **Selection disappointment is larger than it first looks.** The 2007 erratum to Smith and Winkler says the published expected disappointment was understated. Expect the winner's-curse gap to widen as the candidate pool grows.
- **Meta-labeling evidence is mostly controlled experiments.** No peer-reviewed, real-data, net-of-cost intraday test was found, so H3 has no external replication to lean on.
